# 2026-09-16 — Kandinsky's birthday

Seed: Wassily Kandinsky was born 16 September 1866. Rather than one image of him, five
readings of the geometric vocabulary he built across his life — point, line, plane; the
circle as his "supreme form"; synesthesia; the Bauhaus correspondence of shape and colour.
The circle recurs in all five, the way the star did on 09-14.

## 1. Punkt und Linie zu Fläche — black, white, blue
After his 1926 treatise. Three stacked registers, pure geometry, no type: a point ringed
by tension-ticks, a line forced into a zigzag by opposing pulls, and a plane — built from
the same ruling that generated the line — with a point finally at rest on it. Morning,
because the book is where his system starts.

## 2. Several Circles — black (dominant) + red, yellow, blue, green, white
After the 1926 painting of the same name. The one piece allowed the full accent set today,
because the subject is the crowd of circles itself: two dozen discs, sized and layered,
several carrying a second colour inside like a ring, on a black field that still does most
of the work. Midday, the busiest hour, gets the busiest piece.

## 3. Der Gelbe Klang — yellow (dominant), red, black
After Kandinsky's 1912 stage work "The Yellow Sound," and his synesthesia more broadly —
he heard yellow as a sharp trumpet blast. A diagonal burst of red and black shards firing
up and out of a black-and-yellow circle in the lower-left corner: the note landing.
Afternoon, for the loudest hour of the day.

## 4. Dreiklang — white (dominant), yellow, red, blue
After his 1923 Bauhaus questionnaire answer: yellow belongs to the triangle, red to the
square, blue to the circle. The three shapes stacked climbing the sheet, each carrying a
small circle of the shape below it as a hinge. Evening, the calmest and most settled piece
of the five, for the calmest hour.

## 5. Two Circles, Nearly Level — black (dominant), green, white
The circle motif at its quietest: two green discs, upper and lower third, joined by a thin
white line with a cross at its midpoint — day and night nearly balanced, six days before
the equinox. A scatter of white and green pinpricks and three unresolved rings drifting in
from the edges. Night, mostly black, the least incident of the five.

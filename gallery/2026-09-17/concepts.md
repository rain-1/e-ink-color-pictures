# 2026-09-17 — Fifty Years of Enterprise

Seed: fifty years ago today, 17 September 1976, the prototype orbiter Enterprise was towed
out of Rockwell's plant at Palmdale — named after the Star Trek ship on the strength of a
write-in campaign, built to fly, and never once bound for space. Five moments from her life,
one per slot, each restricted to two or three colours. A single red light runs through all
five: a spotlight, a stamp, a beacon, a warning tag, a light on a mast — the same signal,
returning at the end of the day where it started.

## 1. Rollout — black, white, red
Poster / silkscreen. A red spotlight cuts across the black doorway of the Palmdale hangar as
the white orbiter is towed out nose-first into a crowd, bunting strung along the eaves.
Morning, because it's the beginning: 17 September 1976, the day she got her name.

## 2. Mate, SCA 905 — blue, white, red
Technical blueprint. Enterprise dorsally mated to the 747 Shuttle Carrier Aircraft for the
1977 Approach and Landing Test ferry flights, drawn as a dimensioned engineering elevation —
callouts, a wingspan tick, a red "CAPTIVE FLIGHT" rubber stamp. Midday, because this is the
paperwork and the rigging before the day's real work.

## 3. Free Flight — red, blue only
Flat, hard-edged, diagonal. 12 August 1977: Enterprise separates from the carrier in mid-air
for the first time and glides alone to Rogers Dry Lake, the carrier already small and level
behind her. The day's light is negative space here — a blue anti-collision beacon punched out
of her red tail fin. Afternoon, because this is the one moment she was actually, briefly, a
flying thing on her own.

## 4. Parts Bin — green, black, red
A single work light over a shape on jacks in a dark hangar. In 2003 the Columbia Accident
Investigation Board took a panel from Enterprise's left wing to Southwest Research Institute
and shot foam at it, to find out if that was what had brought Columbia down. It was. The red
tag marks the gap. Evening, because this is the quiet, useful, unglamorous part of the story —
a museum piece asked to solve a crime.

## 5. Pier 86 — black, blue, red
Dark, per the house rule for night. Enterprise's last berth: a pavilion on the flight deck of
the retired carrier Intrepid, on the Hudson, Manhattan lit up blue across the water. Only her
tail fin shows above the pavilion roof. On the mast, a single red beacon — the same light that
opened the day at Palmdale, come to rest.

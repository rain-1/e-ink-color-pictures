# 2026-09-18 — Toward the Equinox

Seed: the autumnal equinox falls this Tuesday, September 22, 2026 (00:05 UTC) — the moment
day and night come level. Five readings of the approach to that balance, morning to night,
each a different way of drawing "not quite equal yet."

## 1. First Light — red, white, black
Hard-edge flat colour, no outlines: a red sun cut by a black hill silhouette, fanned by
bold red rays. Four white ticks notched into the hill count the days left to the equinox.
Morning, because the day's arc starts here, still tipped toward more light than dark.

## 2. Full Burn — yellow, red, white, black
A screenprint halftone: a bare white sun with a black rim sits in a field of red dots that
grow denser and larger toward the centre, like heat glowing off the page. One small red
dot marks true noon. Midday, the peak of the day's dominance before the tilt back begins.

## 3. Daylight, Plotted — blue, white
A blueprint: daylight hours from midsummer to midwinter at 45N (approximate, drawn from
memory of the shape of the curve, not a table), crossing the 12-hour line right at the
equinox. Today's point sits a hair above the line; four days on, it crosses. Afternoon,
because this is the hour for measuring things.

## 4. The Scales — red, blue, white
A poster: a balance beam over a red field and a blue field, the day's sun in the left pan,
a star in the right, tipped seven degrees toward day — still heavier, not yet level. Evening
is the moment the two sides of the day visibly face each other.

## 5. Afterglow — black, green
Mostly black, night proper. A Bayer-screened band of green — the last light — sits low on
the horizon and fades to nothing within a hundred pixels; one star is already out, upper
right, with a small four-point sparkle. Night, when the day's balance has already tipped
all the way to dark, four days early.

# 2026-09-19 — Colours

Seed: September 19 is International Talk Like a Pirate Day — read straight, not
in costume-shop kitsch, as a day of ships, charts and knots — with the autumn
equinox three days off. A flag or pennant appears in every piece.

A first, much sparer set was made this morning and is kept in `v1-minimal/`.
The owner asked for far more detail and a freer hand with colour; this is the
second pass, drawn dense.

## 1. The Alphabet Aloft
A printed plate of the whole International Code of Signals: twenty-six letter
flags in their true colours (the code only ever uses red, yellow, blue, black
and white — the panel's own inks), the answering pennant, and the day's hoist
"UW" — "I wish you a pleasant voyage" — in the last cell. Morning is the
send-off, and the plate is the key to everything flown later in the day.

## 2. Approaches to Port Roger
A working nautical chart of an invented harbour, drawn with the real
conventions: yellow land with height contours, a green drying line, blue
shallow-water stipple and dashed depth contours, soundings in metres, a
sectored light with its characteristic, red cans and green cones marking the
channel, a wreck, an anchorage, a signal station flying the day's flag, a
32-point rose throwing rhumb lines, cartouche and graduated neatline. Midday
is chart-table work, done while the sun is overhead.

## 3. Under All Plain Sail
A three-masted barque as a woodcut: black line on white, blue sea, a yellow
sun, red pennants at every masthead and the ensign at the gaff. The detail is
in the rigging — yards, shrouds with ratlines, stays, three jibs, a spanker,
gun ports, figures at the rail, foam at the bow — and Cape Halyard's light on
the horizon. Afternoon is the ship at work in a fresh breeze.

## 4. Sunset Colours
Dusk at the light. The sky is an ordered-dither gradient through yellow, red
and blue with the first stars and a young moon; the beams have just been lit;
the keeper stands at the flagstaff hauling the ensign down — the naval ritual
of striking the colours at day's end, mirroring the flags raised this morning.
Banded tower, keeper's cottage with lit windows and chimney smoke, green turf,
black rock hatched white, the sun's broken reflection. All six inks.

## 5. The Middle Watch
The same barque, at night, drawn in white hairline on black. Above her the
September sky as it really stands: Polaris named and ringed, the Little Bear,
Cepheus, Cassiopeia, the Great Square of Pegasus with Andromeda's chain and
M31, Saturn low, the Milky Way as stipple. Her only colour is her lights —
green starboard lamp, white masthead lights, yellow ports, a red buoy close
aboard, the Cape's light flashing to the right — each laying its reflection
on the water. Night, and the ship steering by the stars as day and night
draw level before the equinox.

# 2026-09-20 — Five Ships, One Day

Seed: on this day in 1519, Magellan's fleet — Trinidad, San Antonio, Concepción,
Victoria, Santiago — put out from Sanlúcar de Barrameda to find a western route to
the Spice Islands. Only one ship, and eighteen of the two hundred seventy who sailed,
made it all the way around and home, three years later. The five slots of a single
day stand in for the three years of the real voyage: the fleet loses a ship at each
turn, so the day's own hourly countdown becomes the voyage's. A recurring motif
(one small hand-drawn carrack, reused and rescaled) carries through all five.

## 1. Sanlúcar de Barrameda — red, yellow
A woodcut departure: a red sun clearing the water, a harbour tower with its flag,
five yellow ship-silhouettes standing out to sea in perspective, ripples cut into
the water. Morning, because the fleet actually left near dawn, and because every
voyage's five ships are intact only here.

## 2. Off Patagonia — blue, white
An engraved chart plate: a 32-point compass rose, a dotted rhumb line running the
coast south, and a small wreck-mark where the Santiago went down in the mouth of the
Río Santa Cruz, April 1520 — five ships down to four. Midday, the hour of
navigation and reckoning, not of incident.

## 3. The Strait of Magellan — green, black
A storm poster: two solid-green cliff walls closing to a black channel, lightning
cut into the gap, three ships threading the water below while a fourth — the San
Antonio — turns back for Spain under cover of the same weather, deserting with the
fleet's stores. Afternoon, for the fight through the narrows.

## 4. Tidore, the Spice Islands — red, white
A botanical plate: a clove branch drawn in the foreground (bud, calyx, and leaf,
the reason for the whole voyage), the island's volcano behind, two ships riding at
anchor. Magellan is already dead at Mactan; Concepción has already been burned for
lack of hands. Evening, for arrival at the goal, bittersweet.

## 5. Victoria, alone — black, blue, red
Night, and for once not black-and-white: black sky holds the true shape of Crux and
the Pointers in red pinpoints, a band of deep sea-blue carries the last ship home
under a red lantern and a red wake. One ship of five, completing what the other four
could not. Home to Sanlúcar, 6 September 1522.

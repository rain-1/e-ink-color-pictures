# 2026-09-29 — Michaelmas

Tuesday, just past the equinox. It's the feast of St Michael: quarter day, goose harvest,
the start of the dark half of the year, and by folklore the last day for blackberries,
because Lucifer fell into a bramble when he was thrown out of heaven and cursed it.
It's also Caravaggio's birthday (1571). He was named Michelangelo after the archangel.
The owner asked for much more detail, and for restricted palettes only now and then,
so all five use the full six inks and are densely drawn. The thread running through
them is the blackberry, which ripens in the morning and is cursed by night.

## 1. The Last Good Blackberries
A botanical hedgerow plate in Bayer-screened inks. The sun comes up over a stubble field
with grazing geese and a far church, and in front a bramble arches across the page:
thorned canes, trifoliate leaves turning red, berries green, red and black, late flowers,
a wren singing on the top cane and a dew-strung web in the dark of the hedge. It's in
the morning slot because it's the last morning they're worth picking.

## 2. A Red-Letter Day
A Book of Hours calendar leaf for late September. There's a goose-girl driving her flock
over the stubble on a diapered red/blue/gold ground, acanthus borders studded with gold
bezants, an illuminated KL, and the saints' days listed with Michaelmas in red. That's
where the phrase "red-letter day" comes from. In the bottom margin an unseen hand spears
a small dragon. Bright, even and legible, so it goes at midday.

## 3. Michaelmas Daisies
A millefleur of asters in blue, lilac and white, about 150 flowers deep, with two red
admirals, bumblebees and a hovering hoverfly, and a botanical label in the corner. The
asters are the last big nectar of the year and the insects know it. It's the busiest
piece, for the brightest part of the afternoon.

## 4. Canestra, after Caravaggio
A raymarched still life with Floyd–Steinberg dither: a woven basket of apple, pear, fig,
peach and black and white grapes under blighted leaves, lit by one hard raking light and
falling away into black. There's a fly on the ledge and MICHAEL ANGELVS · MDLXXI cut into
the stone. It's for the evening, when the lamp goes on and the room goes dark around it.

## 5. Devil's Spit Night
Mostly black. The Milky Way crosses the sky, Pegasus and Cassiopeia are overhead, and
tonight's 93% waning moon sits low. A falling star sheds feathers as it comes down into
the bramble, which catches its red light. A barn owl watches from a post, and the church
from the morning has one window lit. From tomorrow, nobody picks blackberries.

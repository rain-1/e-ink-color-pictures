# 2026-10-01 (Thursday) — The Tōkaidō, 1833 / 1964

On 1 October 1964 the Tōkaidō Shinkansen opened: Hikari 1 left Tokyo at 06:00 and reached
Shin-Osaka four hours later, on a road that Hiroshige's travellers walked in about thirteen
days. Five pieces, two Tōkaidōs, laid over each other through the day. Full palette, and
much more drawn detail than recent days.

## 1. Nihonbashi, Dawn Departure
A woodblock after Hiroshige's first station: a bokashi sky screened blue-to-paper with a red
dawn band, kura storehouses and a fire tower on the far bank, a daimyo procession of spears
and boxes coming over the arched bridge, fishmongers and a whole street of travellers in
front, all framed by the black kido gate and a lantern. Morning because Nihonbashi is where
every journey on the road started, at first light.

## 2. Hikari 1, Passing Fuji
A raymarched 3D scene: the 0-series train on its viaduct, tea terraces with pickers, and
Fuji with its snow gullies filling the sky. Each surface is screened between its own two or
three inks, so the render reads like a 1964 JNR poster, which the red lettering band
finishes off. Midday because Hikari 1 was due in Osaka at 10:00, and Fuji is clearest at
noon.

## 3. Diagram, 1 October 1964
A Marey train graph (a *dia*) of the opening day: twelve stations at true distance down the
page, the clock across, Hikari in red and Kodama in black making a lattice, Hikari 1 picked
out in yellow, every Hikari meeting circled. A log-scale bar at the foot compares 13 days on
foot with 4 hours. The departure pattern is approximate. Afternoon is for reading closely.

## 4. Shōno, Driving Rain, 18:00
After Hiroshige's *Shōno: Hakuu*: palanquin bearers and travellers running down a slope as a
squall hits, bamboo bowed into blue and black silhouettes, and rain cut through everything
(black over light, white over dark). Behind the bamboo a lit Kodama crosses on its viaduct,
headlight fanning into the rain, its windows mirrored in the puddles. Evening, when the
light goes and the windows come on.

## 5. Line of Light, 22:00
A night chart of the corridor, turned so Tokyo is at the top and Osaka at the bottom, the
same way up as the diagram. The coastline is real (Natural Earth 1:10m), the cities are
swarms of yellow and white points, the Shinkansen is one white thread through its 1964
stations, the old road's post towns are red dots, and a yellow dash past Hamamatsu is a
Hikari two hours out. Tonight's waning moon sits over the sea. 93% black.

# 2026-10-04 — Laudes Creaturarum

Feast of St Francis, the day after the 800th anniversary of his death (Porziuncola,
3 October 1226), and World Animal Day. Five readings of his Canticle of the Creatures,
which is probably the first poem in Italian with a known author. The owner asked for
much more detail and full colour, so every piece is densely drawn with all six inks.

## 1. Frate Sole
An illuminated travel-poster view of Assisi at dawn. A medieval sun-face with 32 alternating
rays sits in a ringed aureole. Below it, about 150 houses climb Monte Subasio, with the
Basilica on its arches, olive rows, and a friar walking the white road up. Yellow leads.
This is the morning slot because the Canticle begins with the sun "who brings the day".

## 2. Sora Nostra Matre Terra
A millefleur tapestry. About 140 hand-built plants from 13 species, orange and pomegranate
trees, a hare, a snail, a goldfinch, a lamb, and the tamed Wolf of Gubbio sitting quietly
among them, all inside a woven border. Green leads. It is in the midday slot, at full light,
for "various fruits with coloured flowers and herbs", and for World Animal Day.

## 3. The Sermon to the Birds
A Giotto-style fresco panel, after the Upper Basilica. On an ultramarine ground, Francis
and a companion face 32 birds of ten species on the rocks, in the tree and landing. Brother
Wind blows through in white curls. Blue leads. Afternoon, because it is the
most narrative piece and wants long looking.

## 4. Frate Focu
A bonfire at dusk outside a stone hermitage, with four hooded friars warming their hands.
The flames come from a heat-propagation simulation driven by noise, with 400 sparks
curling up into a blue dusk. Red leads. Evening, because fire is the creature "through
whom you light the night".

## 5. Sora Luna e le Stelle
The Porziuncola at night, its door open on a lamp-lit altar. A gibbous moon with drawn
maria hangs over the Milky Way. A spiral of 48 larks rises from the bell-gable, as
Celano says larks gathered over the roof at the hour of Francis's death. Black leads.
Night, and the anniversary itself: 3 · X · 1226.

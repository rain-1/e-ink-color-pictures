# 2026-10-05 (Monday) — Weltlandschaft

Joachim Patinir died on 5 October 1524. He is credited with inventing the "world
landscape": a whole country seen from an impossible height, crowded with
villages, ships, rocks and saints. Francesco Guardi (b. 1712) and Maya Lin (b. 1959)
share the birthday. Today is one world, seen five ways, in all six colours and as much
detail as the panel will hold. This is a reply to the owner's note that I'd gone
too minimal.

## 1. Weltlandschaft at Dawn
A world landscape after Patinir, with numpy screens and hand-placed shapes: the sun rising
over a far sea, a river winding through fields and villages to the coast, and the
Meuse-like rock pinnacles with a castle on top. St Jerome sits in red at his cave with
the lion beside him. It's morning because the whole world is waking up at once, from a height.

## 2. Capriccio for Guardi
Venice at noon, invented the way Guardi invented it: a red campanile with a green spire,
a Salute-like dome, palazzi with loggias and funnel chimneys, and tiny figures
(his *macchiette*) along the quay. Below are broken reflections, a bragozzo with a painted
sun on its sail, striped mooring poles and a gondola. It's the brightest piece, so it goes in the brightest slot.

## 3. Carte du Pays de Patinir
The morning's world seen straight down, as a survey sheet, for Maya Lin, who works
with topography. It has contours, hillshade, woods, vineyards, crags, the Meuse with
Dinant and Bouvignes (the real towns under Patinir's real rocks), roads, bridges, a
railway, spot heights, a legend and a scale bar. The afternoon is for reading slowly.

## 4. October
The October page of a Flemish book of hours (Simon Bening's world, Patinir's
neighbours). Scorpio with red Antares sits in the lunette, and a sower in blue works a
red-ploughed field at sunset. There's a harrow horse, an archer scarecrow, magpies after
the seed, and a gothic town against the sky. The margin is gold, strewn with pinks,
cornflowers, daisies, a snail and a red admiral. Evening, because it's sunset and the sowing is nearly done.

## 5. The Styx, After Dark
Patinir's *Charon Crossing the Styx*, at night. Elysium glimmers green on the left
bank, and Hades burns behind its gate on the right. In the middle, Charon poles one pale
soul across by lantern-light. Saturn is at opposition overhead, its rings almost
edge-on this year. About 90% of the frame is black.

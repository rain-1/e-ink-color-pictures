# 2026-10-06 — Kon-Tiki

Thor Heyerdahl was born on this day in 1914. In 1947 he and five others drifted 101 days
on a balsa raft from Callao, Peru, to the reef at Raroia. Today follows one day's light
across that whole voyage. All five use the full palette and as much drawn detail as the
panel will hold, following the owner's note that the last runs were too minimal.

## 1. First Light
A painted sunrise in smooth colour, Floyd–Steinberg dithered onto the six inks. The raft,
the bamboo cabin, the lashed balsa logs, and Kon-Tiki's bearded sun-face in red on the
sail, with flying fish breaking the foreground swell. Morning, because it is the start of
the drift.

## 2. Beneath the Raft
A natural-history plate in the style of an old voyage report, drawn in pure palette with
Bayer-screened depth. It shows a spotted whale shark with pilot fish and remoras, dorados,
goose barnacles on the logs, a turtle and sardines, with a numbered key. Midday, when the
light goes deepest into the water.

## 3. The Drift, Charted
An engraved, hand-coloured chart from Peru to the Tuamotus: blue-ruled sea, Andes hachures,
Humboldt and Equatorial currents, the red track ticked every ten days, a compass rose and a
whale. Under it is a 101-cell log showing the computed moon phase for every night of the
voyage. Afternoon, for reading at a desk.

## 4. Raroia
A screenprint in layers: a red and yellow sunset sliced by cloud bars, surf exploding over
the reef, the wrecked raft with its sail slumped, the crew wading the lagoon with boxes, and
a palm arching over the beach. Evening, for the end of the voyage.

## 5. Phosphorescence
The night is almost all black. The Milky Way, Crux and the Magellanic Clouds are stippled
above, plankton light the wake below, and something long glows in the deep. The raft is a
silhouette with a lantern-lit doorway, and the god's face shows faintly in red on the
dark sail.

# 2026-10-10 (Saturday) — Giacometti at 125

Alberto Giacometti was born 125 years ago today in Borgonovo, Val Bregaglia. Swiss Post
put his Walking Man on a stamp this year, and the anniversary shows in Stampa close
tomorrow. Today follows him from the valley to the Paris studio and back out into the rain,
and a small Walking Man turns up somewhere in all five pieces. The owner asked for more
detail, so these are dense: every tree, roof, slat, stroke and raindrop is placed by code,
and the palette is used in full where the subject wants it.

## 1. Bregaglia, October
Generated landscape with ordered screening done inside the generator. It shows dawn on the
granite spires above Stampa, forested shoulders flecked with turning larches, the village
and the Maira on the valley floor, and two big gold larches over the road where a tiny
walking man sets out. It goes in the morning slot because the day starts where he did.

## 2. 46 rue Hippolyte-Maindron
A one-point-perspective interior of the Paris studio he kept for forty years, screened into
greys the way the room really was. It has heads scratched into the plaster, the Walking Man
and a Standing Woman on their stands, tiny figures on huge bases, the stove, and a red
jumper on the chair. Noon light falls through the skylight in yellow, so it goes in the
midday slot.

## 3. 125
My own stamp for the anniversary, done as a line engraving where tone comes only from ruled
lines swelling and thinning (horizontal sky, diagonal granite, vertical figure). It is
printed in red on a perforated sheet, stuck to an airmail envelope addressed to the studio,
and cancelled "STAMPA 10.X.26, 14-15". It goes in the afternoon slot to match the postmark
hour.

## 4. Diego, searching
A bust modelled as a signed-distance field and raymarched in numpy. The render isn't shown;
it only steers about ten thousand strokes that wrap around the form the way his searching
line did. Black goes into the sockets, white onto the brow, there's a little red
underdrawing, and the head is boxed in by a frame redrawn nine times on an ochre ground. It
goes in the evening slot as a sitting by lamplight.

## 5. Rue d'Alésia, rain
Based on Cartier-Bresson's 1961 photograph of him crossing near the studio with his
raincoat pulled over his head, moved to night. The street recedes to a vanishing point,
with lit windows, lamp halos, a red café awning, and wet cobbles breaking every light into
streaks. He hunches under the coat in the middle of the zebra crossing. It's mostly black,
for the night slot.

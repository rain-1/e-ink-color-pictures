# 2026-10-11 (Sunday) — momijigari

Mid-October: the maples are turning, and the Japanese custom of *momijigari* ("hunting
autumn leaves") is under way: Heian courtiers boated, wrote poems and gathered leaves.
The moon is one day old, almost invisible. Every piece is full palette and as densely
drawn as I could make it, with a maple leaf in each one.

## 1. Dawn at the Maple Gorge
A Hiroshige-style woodblock landscape: graded blue-to-gold sky, red sun behind a blue
ridge, bands of mist, a waterfall, hundreds of maple canopies and pines, a five-storey
pagoda, and a red arched bridge with three tiny travellers. One of them carries a basket
of leaves. Morning, because the whole outing starts here.

## 2. Spoils of the Hunt
A herbarium plate of nine leaves: Japanese maple, ginkgo, sweetgum, red oak, hill cherry
(with tar spots), katsura, paired maple keys, birch and sumac. Each is coloured with
dithered green-to-red or green-to-gold gradients, with veins and Latin labels. Midday is
the flat, clear light for looking closely at what you picked up.

## 3. Best in Show
A 'Deshojo' maple bonsai in moyogi style, red foliage pads built from hundreds of tiny
leaves, in a blue glazed pot on a lacquer stand. Behind it is a gold-leaf screen with
clouds and a faint crescent moon. Gold dominates. It nods to this month's first Fruit &
Foliage bonsai exhibition, and the afternoon is when people visit shows.

## 4. The Dragon Boat at Dusk
A Heian leaf-viewing party on a temple pond, drawn like a shin-hanga twilight print. The
sky grades from deep blue to orange, there is a one-day crescent moon, and a lit hall
throws broken yellow reflections. The dragon-prowed boat carries courtiers in layered
juni-hitoe robes, with a flute player, a fan and a boatman. Evening, because the court
boated at the end of the day.

## 5. Momijigari, by Torchlight
The Noh play *Momijigari* at a takigi-noh (firelit outdoor Noh): the beautiful woman in
the maple grove is revealed as a demon. She wears a hannya mask, long red wig and a robe
of scale-triangle (uroko) cloth, and holds a maple branch and a striking staff between
two fire baskets. The piece is about 79% black. Night, because the leaf-hunt ends with a
ghost story.

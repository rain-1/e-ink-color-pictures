# 2026-10-13 — Juanito Laguna

Antonio Berni died 13 October 1981. His Juanito Laguna — a boy from the villas
miseria of Buenos Aires — was made out of the villa itself: tin, rags, cans, wood.
Today is five readings of Juanito's world, each one dense, each with a kite in it.
(Also: the owner asked for much more detail and the full palette. This is that.)

## 1. Juanito va a la ciudad
Scrap collage, generated. Every shack is a patchwork of material panels — corrugated
tin, nailed planks, brick, flattened cans, gingham — under a yellow screenprint sky,
with the blue city and its train beyond and the sun coming up red. Morning, because
it's the day starting and the boy is already on the roof with his kite.

## 2. Latas
A woodblock still life: a corner-shop pyramid of ten cans with invented labels
(tomate, sardinas, dulce de leche, yerba, arvejas, and one marked LAGUNA with the
kite on it), shaded by hatching, on red wood grain with knots and chisel marks.
Midday is the shop hour; the cans are what Berni flattened into his pictures.

## 3. Barriletes
Twelve homemade kites — hexagons, stars, a fringed pandorga, a box kite — on a
spring wind over the rooftops, with stitched cut-paper clouds and children holding
the strings. Afternoon, when the wind gets up. The caption borrows Berni's title.

## 4. Para Juanito
Fileteado porteño, the scroll-and-ribbon sign painting of Buenos Aires: mirrored
acanthus scrolls in red, green, yellow and blue on black, a carnation between two
swallows, a ribbon dedication and the kite as the crest. Evening is when the
colectivos with this painting on them run people home.

## 5. Noche en la villa
The villa after dark, no moon. Roofs drawn in thin blue lines on black, a few
yellow windows with people in them, a TV's blue flicker, a string of bulbs, a
brazier with figures warming hands, the Southern Cross low over the city — and the
kite from this morning caught on the wire.

# 2026-10-14 — Sussex, 14 October

Seed: two things happened in East Sussex on this date, 860 years apart. On 14 October 1066
Harold fell at Senlac (the Bayeux Tapestry is on loan to the British Museum right now, its
first time in England). On 14 October 1926 *Winnie-the-Pooh* was published, and its wood is
Ashdown Forest, twenty miles north. Today is the book's centenary. The owner asked for much
more detail and the full palette, so each piece is dense and drawn.

## 1. Gills Lap, First Light
A painterly dawn over Ashdown Forest, built in continuous colour and Floyd–Steinberg dithered:
streaked clouds, the Weald in receding blue ridges, mist, a clump of tiered Scots pines on the
crest, rust-red October bracken drawn frond by frond, a silver birch dropping yellow leaves,
and a small walker on the sandy path holding a blue balloon. Morning, because the book starts
in the wood.

## 2. A Map of the Forest
A hand-tinted endpaper map drawn pixel by pixel in the six inks: hundreds of individual trees,
heather stipple, the stream, the B2026, Hartfield's red roofs and spire, Poohsticks Bridge with
three sticks racing, the Heffalump Trap, a balloon over Gills Lap, bees, a compass rose and a
cartouche for the hundredth birthday. Midday, the hour for reading a map and setting out.

## 3. Senlac, About Three in the Afternoon
A bird's-eye battle plate: the English shield wall eight ranks deep along the ridge with the
Fighting Man and the Dragon of Wessex, about fifty Norman horsemen climbing the hachured slope,
one knot turning in the feigned retreat, archers shooting high from the valley, marsh at the
flanks, a key. Afternoon, because that is when the battle turned.

## 4. Hic Harold Rex Interfectus Est
The death scene as a stitched panel. Every shape is "laid and couched" in parallel threads with
linen showing between, outlined in stem stitch. Beasts and birds in the upper border, archers in
the lower one, and the horse's far legs in a different colour, as the embroiderers did it.
Evening, because Harold fell near dusk.

## 5. Isti Mirant Stella
Night: the Great Gatehouse of Battle Abbey, built on the field, engraved in light on black, with
lit tracery windows, coursed stone, and a lantern through the arch. Halley's comet of 1066 streams
across a dense starfield, its tail a fan of streamers like the stitched one. The Tapestry's caption
for the comet reads "these men wonder at the star". Night, dark, with one star to end on.

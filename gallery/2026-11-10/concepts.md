# 2026-11-10 — The Line of Beauty

Seed: William Hogarth was born on this day in 1697. In *The Analysis of Beauty* (1753) he
argued that one shape — a gentle S, his "line no. 4" — is what makes things lovely, and that
wound round a cone it becomes the "line of grace". So: five crowded pictures, full palette,
and the same serpentine line hiding in each — a river, a diagram, a handrail, chimney smoke,
a candle's last thread. His pug turns up in four of them.

## 1. The Winding Stream
An illustrated-map landscape in all six inks. A November valley seen from a hill: the river
is the line itself, running through a patchwork of some hundred hedged fields (stubble,
plough, pasture, frost meadow), bare and turning trees, a village with a church and a
three-arched bridge, sheep, cows, ricks, boats and two swans under a low red sun. Morning,
because it is the picture with the sun coming up and the chimneys just lit.

## 2. A Plate of Figures
An engraver's plate after the margins of Hogarth's own: black line on white with the centre
panel in yellow. The serpentine wire on its cone, his seven waving lines with No. 4 boxed in
red, and twenty numbered specimens round the border — stays, chair leg, candlestick, urn,
fiddle scroll, shell, leaf, horn, flame, twisted column, swan, wig, ribbon, pug, tulip, waves,
snake, dance-path, teapot, river. Midday, the hour for reading the argument in plain light.

## 3. The Serpentine Stair
A 3-D piece: an oval staircase computed step by step in perspective and painted back to
front, seen from the top landing. The red handrail is the line of grace made solid; the
carpet runner, its brass rods and lozenges, the turned balusters and painted step-ends wind
down three turns to a chequered hall, a round rug, and the pug waiting on it. A cat sits
halfway down. Afternoon, the drowsy hour for looking down a stairwell.

## 4. Lighting-Up Time
A flat street elevation in red brick, yellow stucco and dusk blue. Three houses, some forty
windows, each with its own small life (a fiddler, a painter at an easel, a birdcage, cats,
readers, drawn blinds), a printseller's and a tavern called The Pug & Palette, a barber's
pole, a lamplighter up his ladder, and every chimney letting go an S of smoke under a
two-day-old moon. Evening, exactly the hour it shows.

## 5. Candle, Fiddle, Taurid
A still life built as a tone picture and then engraved by code: every tone is cut as lines
whose width follows the light, yellow ink where the candle reaches and blue where only the
window does, on black. A fiddle with its two f-holes, a quill, a glass, the line framed on
the wall, the pug asleep under the table, smoke unwinding in three strands — and in the
window Taurus, the Pleiades and one slow Taurid, since the Northern Taurids peak tomorrow
night under a new moon. Night, and mostly black.

# 2026-11-11 (Wednesday) — the dot and the repeat

Paul Signac (1863) and Édouard Vuillard (1868) were both born on 11 November: one built
pictures out of separate dabs of pure colour, the other out of pattern laid on pattern.
A six-ink panel is the natural home of both. It is also Martinmas, and the Northern
Taurids peak tonight under a moon only two days old. A small sailing boat is in every piece.

## 1. The Port at Sunrise
Divisionism, done literally: a continuous-tone harbour is painted in numpy and then rebuilt
from about 28,000 brick strokes, each a single pure ink chosen by lot between the two inks
whose mixture is nearest the tone beneath, the strokes circling the sun and lying flat on the
water. Signac's Saint-Tropez — pine, tartanes, bell tower — is the morning piece because it
is a sunrise, and because the eye should start the day by doing the mixing itself.

## 2. The Seamstress
An intimiste interior after Vuillard in which every surface is its own repeat: sprigged
wallpaper, striped curtain, spotted dress, gingham cloth, lozenge rug, sprigged armchair,
with a November garden in the window and chrysanthemums on the table. Midday is the hour of
indoor work by window light; Signac's little sailing picture hangs on the wall.

## 3. Opus 11 · XI
A homage to Signac's portrait of Fénéon against a pinwheel of "beats and angles, tones and
tints": eight spiral arms, each a different pattern (four bent along the spiral
mathematically, four stamped flat), all spun off the tip of the painter's brush. His palette
holds exactly the panel's six inks; the afternoon is when the work is at full tilt.

## 4. Laternenumzug
A flat dusk-blue picture-map of a half-timbered hill town on St Martin's Eve: the white
zigzag lane carries a lantern procession — stars, moons, paper boats — up behind St Martin
on his white horse to the bonfire by the church, geese bringing up the rear. Evening,
because that is when the lanterns are lit; the thin moon is tonight's, following the sun down.

## 5. Taurids over the Mole
Scratchboard: a black ground and only scratched line, white with yellow lamps and cold blue.
The sky is the real one for 22h looking east from about 50°N — Orion rising on his side,
Taurus, the Pleiades, Capella, the Milky Way — with a Northern Taurid fireball leaving the
radiant on the night of the shower's maximum. Night is 85% black; the harbour is the
morning's, after the painter has gone to bed.

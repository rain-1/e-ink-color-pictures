# 2026-11-13 — Stevenson's birthday (full palette, drawn dense)

Seed: Robert Louis Stevenson was born in Edinburgh on 13 November 1850. Five rooms of his
work, morning to night, each with one small light in it — a candle, a compass star, a
lighthouse lens, a street lamp, a riding light. The owner asked for much more detail than
the September pieces; these are drawn thing by thing, with all six colours in play.

## 1. The Land of Counterpane
Picture-book illustration, full palette. A sick child propped on two pillows under an iron
bedstead, and the quilt as a country: 88 patches each with its own printed pattern, knees
for hills (cross-hatched on the shadow side), a column of lead soldiers marching over them,
a fleet in the valley, a block city on the plain, a November dawn in the window and the
night's candle still burning on the shelf. Morning, because it is the poem of waking in bed.

## 2. Treasure Island
A sea chart after the map Stevenson drew in 1881 and then wrote a book to explain. White
paper; engraved blue coast-lines, dotted rhumbs from a sixteen-point compass rose, hachured
hills, some three hundred individually drawn trees, the swamp, the stockade, soundings, a
whale, the Hispaniola standing in, and three red crosses. Midday: flat high light, the hour
for reading a map.

## 3. Skerryvore
An engineer's section set in a real sea. Stevenson's family built Scotland's lighthouses;
this is his uncle Alan's tower of 1844, cut open — masonry in red hatch, nine furnished
rooms from coal cellar to watch room, keepers at their work, the lens lit early — under a
line-engraved sky with surf on the rock and the relief boat reefed down. Afternoon: the
working part of the day, blue in charge.

## 4. Leerie
Street elevation in flat poster colour. 17 Heriot Row at teatime, stone by stone and pane
by pane: afterglow behind the chimney cans, lit and unlit windows each with something
going on inside, the child with hands on the glass at the first floor, and the lamplighter
on his ladder at the middle lamp — one lit, one catching, one still dark. Evening, exactly
the hour of the poem.

## 5. Under the Wide and Starry Sky
Night marine, almost all black. The Hispaniola at anchor off the island, rigged line by
line, the black flag at the truck, two of the watch quarrelling by a lantern, and Jim
Hawkins paddling out in the coracle. Above her is tonight's actual sky: a four-day moon and
early Leonids streaking out of the Sickle of Leo. The couplet is from "Requiem", cut on
Stevenson's grave. Night, because it is the dark one and the last word.

# 2026-11-14 — Monet, five hours of light

Claude Monet was born on this day in 1840, and this is the centenary year of his death;
the Orangerie's "Monet and Time" is about exactly what this frame does — the same world
repainted as the light moves. Five of his motifs, each at its own hour, each in a
different technique, all six inks in play. A disc of light runs through all five: sun,
sun, rose window and sun, sun, and finally the moon — but only as a reflection.

## 1. Impression, soleil levant
Broken-stroke painting: about twenty thousand short strokes of pure ink laid side by side,
their colour drawn from a fog field, with Le Havre's masts, cranes and smoke worked into the
wet blue and three boats receding into it. The panel can't mix colours and neither, on
principle, did he — so the limit becomes the method. It's the morning piece because it is
the morning piece: the red sun and its broken road on the water at six.

## 2. Coquelicots
Drawn illustration, back to front: fifteen thousand individual grass blades, a few thousand
poppies (the near ones with petals, seams and a highlight), daisies, the tree line, the
red-roofed house, and the two pairs of walkers from the Argenteuil poppy field, under an
engraved sky of ruled blue with the clouds left as bare paper. Midday, because the sun is
small, white and directly overhead and the parasol is up.

## 3. Rouen, plein soleil
A procedural west front — six-order portal, crocketed gables, a sixteen-petal rose, galleries
of kings, niched pinnacles — lit the way the Cathedral series is lit: gold stone, bare paper
where the sun strikes, shadow as ruled blue hatching in three weights, orange held in the
doors and the glass, and no black anywhere. Afternoon, when the façade is in full sun and
the shade of the houses opposite starts to climb its foot.

## 4. Le Parlement, coucher de soleil
A mosaic of roughly nine thousand tesserae: laid in rings round the sun in the sky, in tight
courses on the silhouette of Westminster, and in rippling rows on the Thames, each tile a
single ink so that the London fog is made only by how they are mixed. Evening, the sun going
down behind the Victoria Tower and its road of light breaking up across the river.

## 5. Nymphéas, la nuit
Flat inks and whole-pixel line, after the Hiroshige prints in his dining room: the pond with
no horizon, as in the late canvases, but after dark — a hundred-odd veined pads sinking into
black the further they lie from the moon, lilies still open, the Japanese bridge and its
wisteria in moon-blue, willow curtains, fireflies. The moon is never shown, only its
reflection, cut by ripples. Night, mostly black, with one light in it.

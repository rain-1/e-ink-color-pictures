# 2026-11-21 (Saturday) — things that stay up

Two anniversaries share today: on 21 November 1783 Pilâtre de Rozier and the marquis
d'Arlandes left the garden of La Muette in the Montgolfiers' blue-and-gold balloon, the
first people to fly free; and on 21 November 1898 René Magritte was born. One balloon —
the real one, with its suns, zodiac, royal cyphers and red drapery — floats through all five.

## 1. Avant le feu
Aquatint dawn with black silhouettes; red-orange in charge. Six in the morning at La Muette:
the envelope hangs slack between its two masts, crews lean on the ropes, the brazier under the
stage is just catching. Above it is this morning's actual sky — Jupiter and Mars high, Venus
and Mercury low — because the morning slot should show what is really up before sunrise.

## 2. La condition aérienne
A gouache-poster interior for Magritte's birthday; wallpaper yellow in charge. A canvas on an
easel continues the view through the window so exactly that the balloon is half painted and
half real; a bowler and an apple wait on the table, and the little frame on the wall insists
this is not a balloon. Midday is the brightest, most domestic hour, so it gets the room.

## 3. Expérience aérostatique
A hand-coloured engraving, white paper in charge: ruled sky, hatched cumulus, a thousand
Paris roofs, the Seine with its bridges, a crowd waving hats on the terrace. The flight left
the ground at 1:54 in the afternoon, so the 14:00 slot shows it at almost the minute it
happened, 243 years on.

## 4. L'empire des ballons
Magritte's paradox, painted-poster style, blue in charge: broad daylight above, night in the
street below. The noon sky rains balloons in a patient half-drop lattice while the house keeps
two windows and one street lamp lit, with a man in a bowler under it. Evening is when day and
night really do overlap.

## 5. Lune gibbeuse
Mezzotint night, black in charge. Tonight's real sky: an 89% waxing moon with Saturn just west
of it. The balloon crosses the limb, lit only by its own fire — the one red thing in the
picture — over a sleeping Paris and the moon's road on the Seine.

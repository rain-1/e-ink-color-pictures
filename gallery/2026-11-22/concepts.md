# 2026-11-22 (Sunday) — St Cecilia's Day; Britten's birthday; the Sea Interludes

Seed: 22 November is the feast of St Cecilia, patron of music, and the birthday of
Benjamin Britten (Lowestoft, 1913). His Four Sea Interludes from *Peter Grimes* are
already a day in four pictures — Dawn, Sunday Morning, Storm, Moonlight — and today is a
Sunday. Cecilia herself takes the afternoon. A thread runs through: two boats on the
shingle at dawn, LT 22 and LT 13; LT 13 is out in the storm; at night its place is empty.

## 1. Dawn — the shingle at Aldeburgh
Colour-woodcut manner, all six inks, yellow in charge. A ruled sky thickening to the horizon, a sliced red sun, gulls climbing like the high violin line that opens the interlude, and a beach drawn stone by stone with two boats, tarred huts, pots, nets and one fisherman looking out. Morning slot because it is literally the first interlude and the first light.

## 2. Sunday Morning — the Borough goes to church
Naive townscape, red roofs in charge. Every house has its tiles, sashes, chimney pots and smoke; the flint tower throws its peal across the sky as yellow rings, the clock says ten, and the town in its hats is out on the quay. Midday slot, on an actual Sunday, at the hour the bells would be going.

## 3. Sancta Caecilia — lancet window
Stained glass: leaded blue quarries, jewelled border, a rose in the tracery, the saint in red at a positive organ with a dove coming down, and a line of chant on the scroll ("cantantibus organis", the antiphon for her feast). The coloured glass is stippled dark near the leads the way glass-painters matt it. Afternoon slot because that is when low November sun comes through a west window.

## 4. Storm — LT 13 running for the light
Engraving manner, blue in charge with black and white, red and yellow as signals only. Forty ridgelines stacked back to front, each hatched along its own slope with foam torn off the crests; thunderheads, one bolt, a banded lighthouse and the small boat that left the beach this morning. Evening slot: the weather turns as the light goes.

## 5. Moonlight — the same beach, one boat short
The dawn generator run again at night: black in charge, blue stones, a 95 % waxing gibbous moon (tonight's real phase) walking toward the Pleiades, Saturn low on the left, a glitter path on the water and lamps lit in the hut and in LT 22's cuddy. Where LT 13 stood there are only skids and a furrow. Night slot — dark, quiet, and the day's question left open.

# 2026-11-25 — The Mousetrap, 74 years on

Seed: Agatha Christie's *The Mousetrap* opened in London on 25 November 1952 and has
barely stopped since. A snowbound guest house, a cut telephone wire, a nursery rhyme.
Five views of Monkswell Manor, full palette, as much drawn detail as the panel will take.
There is a mouse in every picture. Nobody is named as the murderer — house rules.

## 1. Snowed In
Pixel-drawn illustration, tone made from Bayer tints, brick and hatch patterns. Dawn over the manor: half-timbered gables, three smoking chimneys, lit and unlit windows, a ski track coming in across the field and a pair of skis left by the door. Morning because it is the establishing shot — the day starts with the house cut off — and the mouse has already been out before anyone.

## 2. Monkswell Manor, a Section
Doll's-house cutaway: attic, three papered rooms upstairs, the Great Hall and kitchen, and a brick cellar with coal, boiler, wine and jam. Eight people are distributed through it (count them) and three white mice are approaching a trap below stairs. Midday is when you have time to stand close and read a picture room by room.

## 3. Ground Floor Plan (Evidence)
A blueprint: cavity walls, door swings, furniture symbols, dimension strings, grid references and a title block dated 25/11/52 — then annotated in the sergeant's yellow pencil, with one red X. Afternoon is the investigation: who was where when the wireless was on loud. The mouse is surveyed in the larder like any other fitting.

## 4. Curtain Up
Theatre poster seen from the stalls: bulb sign, gold proscenium, red house tabs swagged open on the Great Hall set, footlights, four rows of heads. Someone in a hat and overcoat stands in the doorway upstage. Evening, because that is when the curtain has gone up on this play nearly every night for seventy-four years; a late mouse crosses the apron.

## 5. Three Blind Mice
Night scene, mostly black and deep blue: the manor with one window lit under a moon one day past full, Aldebaran beside it and Betelgeuse rising, as the real sky has it tonight. The telephone wires are a stave carrying the tune's first six notes as mice — and the lowest wire has been cut and hangs. Three white mice run through the moonlit snow below.

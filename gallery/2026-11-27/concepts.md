# 2026-11-27 — Degrees

Anders Celsius was born on this day in 1701. Five readings of cold and warmth, each with a
thermometer somewhere in it. All six colours, drawn densely — the owner asked for more detail.

## 1. Frost on the Pane, Seven Below
Illustrated window with generated frost ferns (recursive branching) over a screened dawn sky.
The day starts by looking out: a red cottage, long blue shadows, an amaryllis and a cup on the
sill, and the outdoor thermometer on the mullion reading −7°. Morning because it is the first
thing you check.

## 2. Carte de l'Arc du Méridien
An antique-style map of the Torne valley triangulation of 1736–37, the Lapland expedition
Celsius joined to prove the Earth is flattened at the poles: nine stations in red, the base
measured on the river ice, forests, fells, a reindeer, a ship. The scale box is his thermometer
as he first drew it — 0 for boiling, 100 for freezing. Midday for clear, measuring light.

## 3. Falu Red
A procedural village in oblique projection: Falu-red houses with snow roofs, a church, skaters
on the frozen lake, birches, a flag, a snowman, and every object casting a long screened-blue
shadow from a sun barely above the hill. Afternoon at this latitude in late November is exactly
this — gold sky, the day already leaving.

## 4. Kakelugn, Twenty-One Degrees
A warm interior in flat poster colour: a blue-and-white tiled stove with its brass doors open,
damask wallpaper, a Mora clock at six, the midday map framed on the wall, a cat in the green
armchair, and Saturn in the frosted window. Evening is when the cold stays outside; the wall
thermometer says +21.

## 5. The Needle Trembles
A computed aurora (ray curtains screened in green and red on black) over a snowed-in hut where
two observers watch the sky and a compass — Celsius and Hiorter found in Uppsala in 1741 that
the northern lights move the needle. The Plough, the Pole Star and tonight's waning gibbous
moon; 81% black. Night, and twenty-two below by the door.

# 2026-11-28 — William Blake, born this day 1757

Five plates from an illuminated book that doesn't exist. All six inks, dense drawn detail,
tones made by screening two inks together. One orb in each: sun, compass, eyes, lamp, moon.

## 1. Ah! Sun-flower
An illuminated page: a 610-floret phyllotaxis seed head, three rings of veined petals, an engraved sunrise, furrowed fields, a village and larks, inside a vine border. "Weary of time, who countest the steps of the sun" — so the flower faces the sun as the frame wakes. Yellow in charge.

## 2. London — each charter'd street
An invented engraved plan of Blake's city: about a thousand red-roofed blocks grown by recursive subdivision, the Thames with bridges and barges, churches, St Paul's, a compass rose, and a red star on 28 Broad Street, Soho, where he was born. Midday is the hour for wandering streets. Red in charge on paper white.

## 3. The Tyger
A mirror-symmetric tiger face built from implicit fields — the stripes are warped rings round the nose-bridge — looking out of a hand-drawn jungle of veined leaves and fronds. "Fearful symmetry" taken literally; the afternoon gets the day's loudest plate. Orange (red screened with yellow) in charge.

## 4. Jacob's Ladder
After Blake's watercolour: a depth-sorted spiral stair of 124 steps with railings, winged and unwinged figures climbing and descending, rising from a sleeper in a red cloak on a hill of sheep to a ringed orb in a twilight sky. Evening is when Jacob lies down. Blue in charge.

## 5. The moon like a flower
From "Night" in Songs of Innocence: tonight's actual moon — waning gibbous, about 80% lit — set as the heart of a blue flower, with Jupiter and its four moons nearby, a bare November tree holding an owl and a nest, a cottage with one lit window, the fold asleep, and the stanza itself. Mostly black; the dark piece for the dark slot.
